01Your Rights

Your Rights in Ireland

Clear, factual information about what you are legally entitled to as an international protection applicant — and what nobody has the right to demand from you.

You Are Entitled To Dignity

Under Irish and EU law, international protection applicants have the right to adequate accommodation, healthcare, and a dignified standard of living. No one can lawfully deny you basic amenities because you are an immigrant.

Did a hostel or centre ask you for a doctor's letter for something basic?

There is no legal requirement in the Reception Conditions Regulations for a GP letter to approve basic items like a fan, a room change for comfort, or standard living equipment. The only formal medical letter protocol applies to transfer requests between centres — and even then, it requires a consultant's letter, not a GP note.

The Law That Protects You

Ireland's obligations to people seeking international protection come from the European Communities (Reception Conditions) Regulations 2018 (S.I. No. 230/2018). This law implements the EU Reception Conditions Directive and sets minimum standards for accommodation, healthcare, food, and living conditions.

Key Legal Principle

Regulation 4(1): You are entitled to material reception conditions (accommodation, meals, money) "whereby they do not have sufficient means to attain an adequate standard of living." The State must ensure your accommodation guarantees "a dignified standard of living" and "adequate living space."

Medical Letters: What Is — and Isn't — Required

Important Truth

There is NO legal or regulatory requirement for a GP letter to approve basic amenities like an electric fan, a comfortable room temperature, or standard living equipment. If a hostel or centre demands a doctor's letter for something basic, they are enforcing an internal policy, not a law.

The ONLY Official Medical Letter Protocol

IPAS has one formal protocol involving medical letters: the Protocol for Medical Transfer Requests, introduced in January 2024. This applies only to:

  • Requests to move from one accommodation centre to another
  • Where the move is essential for health reasons
  • Requires a letter from a hospital consultant (not a GP)
  • The letter must state the move is essential, not just preferable
  • IPAS then reviews this with an Independent Medical Referee

Even for transfers, many requests are refused unless there is a significant medical vulnerability or safety concern.

Medical Letters: Quick Reference

Situation Letter Required? What the Law Says
Requesting a fan for your room No Basic living amenity. No statutory requirement.
Requesting a room change within centre No (in most cases) Centre manager discretion; not a medical matter.
Requesting transfer to another centre for health Yes — from a hospital consultant Protocol for Medical Transfer Requests (Jan 2024)
Requesting transfer for work/family No (but likely refused) IPAS policy: transfers only for "exceptional circumstances"
Accessing healthcare / GP registration No You have a legal right to healthcare under Reg. 18.

What "Suitable Accommodation" Means

Since January 2024, HIQA (Health Information and Quality Authority) independently inspects permanent IPAS centres against the legally binding National Standards for Accommodation. These standards cover:

Living Space

Adequate room size, privacy, ventilation, and temperature control. Rooms should be safe and habitable.

Food & Facilities

Nutritious meals or self-catering facilities. Access to clean drinking water and food storage.

Safety & Safeguarding

Fire safety, protection from abuse, and procedures to keep vulnerable people safe.

Special Needs

Assessment and response to disabilities, mental health needs, pregnancy, and family circumstances.

What the Ombudsman Has Said

The Office of the Ombudsman has recommended that protection applicants should have own-door accommodation with adequate living space and access to their own cooking facilities. While this ideal is not yet fully realised, it is the official benchmark against which complaints are judged.

Emergency vs. Permanent Accommodation

As of early 2026, IPAS operates 312 properties across Ireland, but only approximately 49 are permanent centres. The remainder are emergency or pre-reception facilities. HIQA only inspects permanent centres. If you are in emergency accommodation, your remedy is through the complaints process and the Ombudsman — not HIQA inspection. This distinction is critical.

Your Right to Complain

You have a legal right to make a complaint without fear of retaliation. The process is:

01

Talk to your Centre Manager

Try to resolve the issue informally first. If that fails, make a formal written complaint. They must respond within 14 days.

02

Escalate to IPAS

Email ipasinbox@justice.ie with your name, TRC number, and full details of your complaint.

03

Contact the Ombudsman

If IPAS does not resolve your complaint, you can go to the Office of the Ombudsman online, by post, or by phone at 01 639 5600. You must do this within 12 months.

04

Ombudsman for Children

If your complaint relates to a child, contact the Ombudsman for Children's Office instead.

Retaliation Is Not Allowed

Your entitlement to accommodation cannot be reduced or withdrawn simply because you made a complaint. If this happens, contact the Ombudsman immediately and document everything.

What You Receive Weekly

As an international protection applicant in Direct Provision, you are entitled to:

  • Daily Expenses Allowance: €38.80 per week for adults
  • Child allowance: €29.80 per week per child
  • Child Benefit: If you have children, you may also be entitled to Irish Child Benefit

Note: If you take up paid employment, your Daily Expenses Allowance may be reduced proportionally. As of late 2025, the government proposed requiring contributions toward accommodation costs from those earning above certain thresholds. Check gov.ie for current rules.

Your Right to an Interpreter

You have a legal right to interpretation in your asylum interviews with the International Protection Office (IPO), in medical settings, and in legal proceedings. If you are not offered an interpreter, ask for one. Do not let anyone pressure you to proceed without adequate interpretation.

Travel Restrictions

Protection applicants generally cannot leave Ireland without permission while their application is being processed. If you leave without permission, your application may be deemed withdrawn. If you need to travel for an emergency (e.g., family death, serious illness), contact the International Protection Office before making any travel arrangements.

Tip: Document Everything

If you are being asked for unreasonable documentation (like a GP letter for a fan), ask the staff member to show you the written policy or regulation that requires it. If they cannot, that tells you it is not a legal requirement. Keep copies of all letters, emails, and notes of conversations.

03Health

Your Health, Your Right

Everyone in Ireland's international protection system has the right to healthcare — including free GP visits, hospital care, and prescription medicines.

Your Healthcare Rights

Under Regulation 18 of the Reception Conditions Regulations 2018, you have the right to:

  • Emergency healthcare
  • Treatment for serious illnesses
  • Mental health care assessed as necessary
  • Other healthcare to maintain your health

Good News: Prescriptions Are Free

If you live in Direct Provision and have a medical card, you do not pay prescription charges. Normally medical card holders pay a small charge per item, but this is waived for IPAS residents.

How to Access Healthcare

01

Get a Medical Card

Apply through the HSE. If you live in Direct Provision, you are entitled to a medical card. Ask your centre manager or GP for help with the application. You can also apply online at hse.ie.

02

Register with a GP

Most IPAS centres have a GP who visits regularly, or residents are assigned to a local GP practice. If GP lists are full, the HSE will arrange access to a GP with availability in your area. This is free with your medical card.

03

Hospital Care

You are entitled to free care in public hospitals. If you need to go to hospital, your GP will refer you. In an emergency, go to A&E or call 112/999.

04

Dental & Eye Care

Medical card holders get limited free dental and optical services: basic extractions, one eye test every 2 years, and limited glasses/contacts subsidy. Major dental work (crowns, bridges) is generally not covered. Ask your GP or local health centre for a referral.

Requesting an Interpreter

You have the right to an interpreter for GP and hospital appointments. Ask when booking. Do not let anyone tell you to 'bring a friend' instead. Professional interpretation is your right and ensures accuracy.

Mental Health Matters

Living in the protection system can be stressful, lonely, and frightening. Asking for help is a sign of strength, not weakness. You have the right to mental health support, and it will not affect your asylum application.

Talk to Your GP Your GP can refer you to counselling, psychiatry, or psychology services. This is free with your medical card.
HSE YourMentalHealth Helpline Free national mental health helpline. Available 24/7. You can also text 50808. 1800 111 888
SPIRASI For survivors of torture and severe trauma. Therapy, rehabilitation, and medico-legal support. 01 838 9664
Counselling in DP Some centres have on-site counselling. Ask your centre manager or welfare officer what mental health services are available.

Your Health Information Is Confidential

What you tell your doctor is private. Your GP cannot share your medical information with IPAS, the International Protection Office, or the Department of Justice without your consent — except in very rare situations where there is a serious risk to life. Seeking mental health support will not negatively affect your asylum claim.

Health Screening on Arrival

When you first arrive in Ireland, you are offered voluntary health screening at the reception centre. This checks for:

  • Tuberculosis (TB)
  • Hepatitis
  • HIV
  • Vaccination status
  • Other health needs

This screening is voluntary and confidential. The results do not affect your asylum application.

Taking Care of Your Health Day-to-Day

Sleep

Good sleep is essential. If noise, temperature, or room conditions are affecting your sleep, document it and raise it with your centre manager. Poor sleep affects mental and physical health.

Food & Nutrition

If you have dietary needs (religious, medical, allergies), tell your centre manager. Centres are required to cater for dietary requirements.

Movement

Physical activity reduces stress. Walking, stretching, or any exercise you enjoy helps. Many centres have outdoor space — use it if you can.

Connection

Isolation harms health. Connect with others in your centre, local community groups, or support organisations. Clare Immigrant Support Centre runs outreach to Clare Lodge.

Sexual & Reproductive Health

You have the right to access sexual health services, maternity care, and family planning. These services are confidential and free with your medical card.

  • Maternity care: Fully free for medical card holders. Tell your centre manager early if you are pregnant so they can plan appropriate accommodation.
  • Contraception: Free for women aged 17–35. Discuss with your GP.
  • STI testing: Free and confidential at public sexual health clinics. Find your nearest clinic at hse.ie/services/sexual-health.

Children's Health

Children in IPAS are entitled to the same health services as all children in Ireland: public health nurse visits, developmental checks, school health screenings, vaccinations, and specialist referrals. Ensure your children are registered with a GP.

Addiction Services

If you or someone you know is struggling with alcohol or drugs, HSE addiction services are free and confidential. Your GP can refer you, or contact the HSE Drugs & Alcohol Helpline: 1800 459 459.

04Legal Aid

Legal Aid & Advocacy

You have the right to legal help. Whether it's your asylum case, a complaint about your accommodation, or a problem at work, there are people who will help you for free.

Free Legal Help for Asylum Seekers

If you are in the international protection process, you are entitled to free legal aid through the Legal Aid Board and its Refugee Legal Service (RLS).

Refugee Legal Service (RLS) Free legal representation for international protection applicants. They can help with your asylum application, appeals, and judicial reviews. 0818 615 200
Legal Aid Board General civil legal aid — family law, housing, employment, and more. Free if you cannot afford a private solicitor. 0818 615 200
FLAC Free Legal Advice Centres. Drop-in clinics across Ireland for basic legal advice. Independent and confidential. 01 906 1010
Irish Refugee Council Information, advocacy, and casework support. Not a law firm, but they know the system and can guide you. 01 764 5854

The International Protection Office (IPO)

The IPO processes your asylum application. They are not your advocate — they are the decision-maker. You must cooperate with them, attend interviews, and respond to requests. If you move, tell them your new address immediately.

info@ipo.gov.ie · ipo.gov.ie

The International Protection Appeals Tribunal (IPAT)

If the IPO refuses your application, you can appeal to IPAT. You have 15 working days from the date of the refusal letter to submit your appeal. Do not miss this deadline. Contact the Refugee Legal Service immediately if you receive a refusal.

protectionappeals.ie

Your Temporary Residence Certificate (TRC)

Your TRC is the plastic card you received when you claimed asylum. It is your ID in Ireland. Keep it safe. You need it to: register with a GP, collect your PPS number, access services, and prove your status. If you lose it, contact IPAS immediately.

How to Complain About Your Accommodation

If you believe your centre is not meeting standards, treating you unfairly, or asking for unreasonable things (like a doctor's letter for a basic fan), you have a legal right to complain. Here is the proper process:

01

Centre Manager (Informal)

Speak to the manager first. Many issues can be resolved quickly. Keep a note of what was said and when.

02

Centre Manager (Formal, Written)

If informal discussion fails, submit a written complaint. The manager must respond within 14 days. Use the centre's complaint form if they have one. Keep a copy.

03

IPAS Customer Service Team

Email ipasinbox@justice.ie with: your full name and TRC number; the name of your centre; a clear description of the problem; copies of any previous correspondence.

04

Office of the Ombudsman

If IPAS does not resolve your complaint, escalate to the Ombudsman. You must do this within 12 months.

Online: ombudsman.ie
Phone: 01 639 5600
Post: Office of the Ombudsman, 6 Earlsfort Terrace, Dublin 2, D02 W773

05

Ombudsman for Children (OCO)

If your complaint involves a child, contact the OCO at oco.ie or 01 865 6800.

What to Include in Your Complaint

  • Your name, TRC number, and contact details
  • The name and address of your accommodation centre
  • What happened, when, and who was involved
  • What you want to happen now
  • Copies of any letters, emails, or photos that support your case

When to Contact a Solicitor

  • Your international protection application has been refused
  • You want to appeal to the International Protection Appeals Tribunal (IPAT)
  • You are facing deportation or removal
  • You want to apply for judicial review
  • You have been a victim of crime and need legal support
  • You have an employment or housing dispute

Important: Don't Miss Deadlines

Legal deadlines in the asylum system are strict. If you receive a refusal letter, you usually have 15 working days to appeal. Do not wait. Contact the Refugee Legal Service or a solicitor immediately.

Warning: Bogus Legal Advisors

Be careful of people who claim they can 'fast-track' your asylum claim or guarantee a positive result for money. Only registered solicitors and the Refugee Legal Service can legally represent you. If someone asks for cash to 'fix' your case, they are likely fraudulent. Report suspicious activity to the Gardaí or MRCI.

Know Your Rights at Work

If you have Labour Market Access Permission, you have the same employment rights as any other worker in Ireland:

  • Minimum wage (€13.50 per hour as of Jan 2025 — check gov.ie for current rate)
  • Paid holidays
  • Safe working conditions
  • Protection from unfair dismissal (after qualifying period)
  • Right to join a trade union

If your employer is exploiting you — not paying you properly, making you work unsafe hours, or threatening you because of your immigration status — contact MRCI (01 889 7570) or the Workplace Relations Commission (WRC) (059 917 8990) before confronting your employer. If you are undocumented, seek advice first.

Remember

If your employer threatens to report your immigration status because you asserted your employment rights, this may be penalisation and you can report it to MRCI or the WRC. However, if you are undocumented, seek advice before making a complaint to understand your risks.
